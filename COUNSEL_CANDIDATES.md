# Candidate Counsel Shortlist

Researched 2026-09-20. Tiered by fit for an early-stage, affiliate/comparison-site product (not a sportsbook operator) - prestige and firm size are not the same thing as fit here, so the "best-known" firms aren't necessarily ranked first. None of these have been contacted yet; verify current practice focus and get a quote before committing, and use each firm's own site to find the right intake contact rather than a name/email I'd have to guess.

## Tier 1 - closest fit to our actual posture (affiliate/software provider, not operator)

These came up specifically for representing **marketing affiliates and software providers** in gaming, not just sportsbook operators - which is the more relevant experience for a comparison/analytics product with affiliate links.

- **[Womble Bond Dickinson - Gaming](https://www.womblebonddickinson.com/us/sectors/gaming)** - explicitly represents "marketing affiliates" and "software providers" in the gaming sector, alongside operators. Good first call given our exact business model.
- **[Saiber LLC - Gaming Law](https://www.saiber.com/services/regulatory-compliance/gaming-law)** (NJ) - has represented affiliate marketing organizations specifically on negotiating/drafting marketing and referral agreements for interactive gaming and sports-wagering operators. Directly relevant to our affiliate-fee business model.
- **[Walters Law Group](https://www.firstamendment.com/online-gaming/)** (Lawrence G. Walters) - boutique, explicitly covers affiliate marketing / influencer / SEO compliance vetting for online gaming. Likely more startup-accessible than a large firm.
- **[Ifrah Law](https://www.ifrahlaw.com/practice-area/online-gaming-sports-betting/)** - Chambers-USA-ranked specifically in Gaming & Licensing Law, boutique-focused on iGaming/sports betting across the full business lifecycle rather than a generalist practice bolted onto a big firm. Good balance of specialization and (likely) accessibility.

## Tier 2 - established gaming-law practices (broader, likely pricier, strong regulatory depth)

Good if Tier 1 doesn't pan out, or if you want a firm that's helped draft actual state sports-betting regulations (useful for the jurisdiction-analysis deliverable specifically).

- **[Greenberg Traurig - Sports Betting](https://www.gtlaw.com/en/capabilities/gaming/sports-betting)** - 15+ years representing gaming/sports-betting operators; attorneys have helped draft state sports-betting regulations directly.
- **[Dickinson Wright - Gaming Law](https://www.dwlaw.com/practice-areas/gaming-law)** - regulatory/compliance counsel, helped draft gaming legislation.
- **[Nelson Mullins - Gaming](https://www.nelsonmullins.com/services/gaming2)** - counsels sports-betting operators through state/federal regulatory complexity.
- **[Duane Morris - Gaming](https://www.duanemorris.com/practices/gaming.html)** - advises compliance committees of sports-wagering operators on upcoming regulations/reporting.
- **[Orrick - Gaming and Gambling](https://www.orrick.com/en/Practices/Gaming-and-Gambling)** / **[Brownstein - Gaming](https://www.bhfs.com/service/gaming/)** - large, deep-bench gaming practices; likely the most expensive of this list.

## Prediction-market / CFTC specialists (for the separately-scoped Kalshi/Polymarket question)

The RFP already flags this as possibly needing different counsel than the sports-betting work above - these firms are specifically tracking the CFTC/state jurisdictional fight over event contracts, which is the exact question our Phase 2 roadmap needs answered.

- **[Stinson LLP](https://www.stinson.com/newsroom-publications-sportsbooks-or-commodity-exchanges-the-rising-legal-tensions-between-sports-betting-and-prediction-markets)** - published analysis specifically on the sports-betting-vs-prediction-market tension. Most directly on-point of anyone found.
- **[Holland & Knight](https://www.hklaw.com/en/insights/publications/2026/02/prediction-markets-at-a-crossroads-the-continued-jurisdictional-battle)** - actively tracking the state-vs-CFTC jurisdictional battle (Nevada, Massachusetts, Tennessee enforcement actions against Kalshi).
- **[Sidley Austin](https://www.sidley.com/en/services/entertainment-sports-and-media/gaming-and-sports-betting)** - one of the few firms with **both** a gaming/sports-betting practice and active CFTC prediction-market rulemaking coverage, meaning they could plausibly handle the whole RFP scope (items 1-9) under one roof. Also the largest/priciest name on this list - worth a call specifically to ask if that dual coverage is worth the premium over hiring two firms.
- **[Lowenstein Sandler](https://www.lowenstein.com/news-insights/publications/client-alerts/cftc-and-kalshi-announce-enforcement-actions-targeting-prediction-markets-fctm)** / **[Norton Rose Fulbright](https://www.nortonrosefulbright.com/en-us/knowledge/publications/ad8a494a/prediction-markets-at-a-crossroads-preemption-enforcement-and-rulemaking)** - both publishing current analysis on CFTC/Kalshi enforcement and DOJ preemption litigation; reasonable alternates to Stinson/H&K.

## Suggested approach

1. Start with 2-3 from **Tier 1** - they match our actual business model best and are more likely to give a startup-friendly fixed-fee quote for the initial classification memo.
2. Ask each one directly (per the RFP's own qualifying questions) whether they can also cover the prediction-market question, or would refer out - **Sidley** is the one firm here plausibly able to do both, everyone else in Tier 1/2 will likely refer that piece out to someone like Stinson or Holland & Knight.
3. Don't contact the prediction-market specialists yet - that's explicitly a Phase 2 question in the PRD, not blocking MVP launch. Worth knowing who they are now so you're not starting from zero later.

**I have not verified current pricing, availability, or whether these firms are actually taking new startup clients** - that's exactly what the RFP's own qualifying questions (Section 3) are for. Treat this as a call list, not a commitment.
